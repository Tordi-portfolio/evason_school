Academics

The curriculum, the pathways and exactly how we grade.

Our approach to teaching

The Evason curriculum follows the Nigerian national curriculum and prepares students for BECE, WAEC and NECO, enriched with practical work, projects, reading and digital skills. We plan every term in detail: a scheme of work for each subject, weekly lesson plans, scheduled tests and a clear revision programme.

Lessons follow a familiar rhythm. Teachers recap the previous lesson, introduce new material with worked examples, give students time to practise, and finish with a short check for understanding. Homework is meaningful, limited and always marked. When a student falls behind, form teachers and heads of department arrange extra help early, before a small gap becomes a large one.

Reading is the foundation of everything else, so every junior class has a timetabled library period, and every senior student keeps a reading log. Mathematics and English receive extra periods each week because they open the door to every other subject.

How we assess

In every subject, every term, a student earns marks from three components:

ComponentMarks
First continuous assessment test20
Second continuous assessment test20
End-of-term examination60
Total per subject100

The portal adds the three scores, awards the grade, and calculates the student’s overall total, average and position in class.

Reading your result sheet

Each result sheet shows, for every subject, the first test, second test and examination scores, the subject total and the grade. Below the table you will find the student’s overall total, the average across subjects, the overall grade and the position in class.

A position of 3rd out of 41 means that two students in the class scored a higher overall total. Positions are calculated only from results the school has published, so they are always fair and complete. If you believe a mark has been recorded wrongly, contact the examinations office through the school and it will be checked against the original script.

Junior Secondary School, JSS1 to JSS3

A broad foundation for every learner, ending with the BECE.

Junior secondary students follow a wide programme so that nobody narrows their choices too early. Classes are mixed ability, with extra support and extension tasks built into lessons.

  • English Language
  • Mathematics
  • Basic Science
  • Basic Technology
  • Social Studies
  • Civic Education
  • Computer Studies
  • Agricultural Science
  • Business Studies
  • Christian Religious Studies
  • Cultural and Creative Arts
  • Physical and Health Education
  • Igbo Language
  • Home Economics

In JSS3 we add a dedicated BECE preparation programme with past questions, timed practice papers and individual feedback, plus career talks and a subject-choice workshop for parents and students before SS1.

Senior Secondary School, SS1 to SS3

Three pathways, one shared core, and a clear route to university.

Core subjects for everyone

English Language, Mathematics, Civic Education and Computer Studies are taken by all senior students, because clear writing, numeracy, citizenship and digital competence matter whatever path a student chooses.

Science pathway

Physics, Chemistry, Biology and Further Mathematics, with regular practical sessions. Designed for students aiming at medicine, engineering, pharmacy, computer science, agriculture and the other sciences.

Arts pathway

Literature in English, Government, Christian Religious Studies, Geography and Economics. Designed for future lawyers, writers, diplomats, teachers, historians and social scientists.

Commercial pathway

Financial Accounting, Commerce, Economics and Business-related electives. Designed for future accountants, entrepreneurs, bankers, managers and analysts.

SS3 students sit termly mock examinations under real conditions, attend subject clinics on weekday evenings and Saturdays, and receive one-to-one guidance on university applications, JAMB registration and scholarship opportunities.

Digital learning

Technology at Evason serves teaching and does not replace it. Students learn keyboard skills, word processing, spreadsheets and presentation tools, then move on to research methods, online safety and programming fundamentals. Senior students complete a project each year, such as a budgeting spreadsheet, a data collection study or a simple web page.

The school portal is part of this learning. Students check their own results, learn to read a report, and set goals for the next term in conversation with their form teacher.

Support for every learner

Our guidance counsellor meets students individually and in groups to help with study skills, wellbeing, subject choices and career planning. Students who need extra challenge join enrichment groups, and students who need additional help attend supervised catch-up sessions at no extra charge.

We keep parents informed through termly reports, open evenings and quick calls whenever a teacher notices something that deserves attention, good or concerning.
